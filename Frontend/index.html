👁️ Derived Ocular Perfusion Variables

When IOP is available from clinical reference data, blood-pressure-derived variables can be combined with IOP to calculate simplified ocular perfusion pressure estimates for research exploration.

MAP ≈ DBP + (SBP − DBP) / 3

MOPP ≈ (2 / 3) × MAP − IOP

DOPP = DBP − IOP

These formulas are simplified research representations and should not be treated as diagnostic thresholds.

MODULE 12

Research Context

Scientific motivation, research gap, limitations and future development.

✅ Established Components
  • Blood pressure is a relevant vascular variable in glaucoma research.
  • Ocular perfusion pressure has been investigated in relation to glaucoma and glaucoma-related outcomes.
  • Retinal and optic nerve head vascular characteristics can be investigated using specialized ophthalmic imaging.
  • PPG provides a non-invasive peripheral optical pulse waveform.
  • Clinical glaucoma assessment uses multiple complementary measurements rather than a single low-cost sensor.
⚠️ Not Established by This Prototype
  • PPG alone is not a validated glaucoma diagnostic biomarker.
  • MAX30102 does not directly measure ocular blood flow.
  • MAX30102 does not directly measure blood pressure.
  • Finger SpO₂ should not be interpreted as retinal oxygen saturation.
  • The prototype cannot replace OCT, OCTA, tonometry, visual field testing or ophthalmic examination.
🔍 Research Gap

A potential research direction is to investigate whether combinations of inexpensive peripheral physiological signals can provide incremental information that complements established clinical assessment.

The important question is not whether an IoT sensor can directly diagnose glaucoma, but whether measurable physiological patterns contain useful additional information for screening-support research.

CENTRAL RESEARCH QUESTION

Can low-cost peripheral physiological signals provide incremental information useful for identifying individuals who may benefit from formal ophthalmic evaluation?

Phase 1

Prototype

  • Build sensing architecture
  • Acquire PPG
  • Integrate validated BP source
  • Develop mobile/web interface
Phase 2

Data Study

  • Recruit appropriate participants
  • Collect de-identified data
  • Pair physiological and clinical variables
  • Perform statistical analysis
Phase 3

Validation

  • Train candidate models
  • Use independent validation
  • Report uncertainty
  • Assess clinical usefulness
🛡️ Research Limitations
  • Simulation data cannot establish clinical validity.
  • Peripheral physiological signals are affected by motion, temperature, sensor placement and individual variability.
  • Associations between vascular variables and glaucoma do not necessarily establish causation.
  • Clinical measurements such as IOP and OCT/OCTA require appropriate professional equipment and expertise.
  • A real ML model requires representative data and rigorous validation.
